# CLAUDE.md

## About

This project is a **demographic study of the human race** — its ancestry, its present
populations, and its migration patterns. The aim is to trace where people *came from*,
understand who they *are today*, and discern where they are *going*.

It is at once historical, contemporary, and forward-looking:

- **History / Ancestry** — how peoples descend, branch, and recombine over time.
- **Present populations** — who lives where now, and the cultures they carry.
- **Migration patterns** — how the human family has moved, is moving, and is likely to move.

### Theological orientation

The work is grounded in **Catholic theology**. Its starting conviction is that humanity
is **one race** — a single human family — descended from **Adam**, re-rooted through
**Noah**, and progressively dispersed and differentiated over the generations. The many
peoples of the earth are not separate kinds but, in the image of St. Paul's teaching,
**different members of one body**: distinct in gift, role, and expression, yet united in
one shared origin and dignity.

A core premise is that **faith and reason do not contradict**. The biblical account of
common descent and dispersion and the findings of science (genetics, archaeology,
linguistics, anthropology, history) are treated as **complementary witnesses to the same
reality**. Where the data and the Scriptures meet, the project seeks the correlation
rather than the conflict.

### The deeper goal

Beyond mapping populations, the project asks a contemplative question:
**what is God doing with the human race, and where is its heart moving?**

To read the heart of humanity at the *macro* level, one must attend to the *micro* — the
particular vessels: the individual peoples, mixes, and communities, and the way each
**expresses itself through culture**. Culture is treated as a primary signal precisely
because raw census/demographic data is limited; how a people creates, works, worships,
and lives reveals what numbers alone cannot. Everyone carries gifts and produces
something, and those gifts and creations show up legibly in the patterns of demographics
and migration.

### Approach and scope

The study moves from **macro to micro**:

1. **Macro first** — broad, census-style groupings of major populations (the way a
   national census organizes people into large categories).
2. **Micro trends next** — finer-grained communities and admixtures (e.g. Irish–German,
   Irish–African-American), where genuinely distinct demographic and cultural patterns
   emerge.

We begin with **America**. It is deliberately a hard first case — already a complex,
deeply mixed society — but it offers a rich proving ground. For each group we want the
**whole arc of origin**: not just a present-day label but the historical lineage behind
it (for example, tracing African-American populations back through their predominantly
West African roots and the specific peoples and movements that fed into them).

### What we want to track

- **Demographic data** — populations, distributions, movements over time.
- **Origin / ancestry** — the historical lineage and source peoples behind today's groups.
- **Culture** — how peoples express themselves: what they create, value, and practice
  (used as a key to identity where bare data falls short).
- **Correlations** — links between migration, culture shifts, economics, skills/vocation,
  and other forces (including factors not yet anticipated).

### Guiding principles

- Treat **all peoples** with equal dignity — one body, many members.
- Pursue **correlation, not contradiction**, between Scripture and science.
- Read the **macro through the micro** — the heart of the whole through the detail of each part.
- Gather **as much real data as possible**, and let culture fill in what numbers cannot.
- Keep the orienting question always in view: **who are the people of today, where did
  they come from, and where are they going?**
