# Roadmap

This roadmap is organized as **stacking layers**. Each phase adds a new kind of data on
top of the previous one; the real payoff comes in the final phase, where the layers are
tied together to read correlations across demography, genetics, history, and culture.

The progression deliberately moves from the **macro** (broad, census-style groupings) to
the **micro** (finer admixtures and cultural detail) — and from **readily available
data** toward **harder, deeper, more interpretive data**.

We begin with **America** as the first geography. It is a deliberately hard first case —
already complex and deeply mixed — but a rich proving ground.

---

## Phase 0 — Scaffold

Establish the foundation before gathering data.

- Set up repository structure (data folders, sources/citations, analysis notebooks).
- Fix America as the first geography.
- Define the **macro groups** up front, mirroring census categories so later layers line up.
- Establish a per-source convention for license + citation tracking from day one.

## Phase 1 — Macro census baseline *(readily available data)*

Build the big-picture demographic map of America — the skeleton everything else hangs on.

- Race / ethnicity / ancestry, geography, language, religion, income, over time.
- All free and well-structured (see Tier 1 in `DATA_SOURCES.md`).
- Deliverable: a clean macro picture of who lives where, and how that has shifted.

## Phase 2 — Modern genetic layer

Overlay present-day population genetics onto the census groups.

- Admixture and allele frequencies by population.
- Connect census labels (e.g. "African-American") to actual ancestral components.
- Sources: Tier 2.

## Phase 3 — Historical / origins layer

Add the "where they came from" arc — the documentary history behind today's groups.

- Immigration flows, the slave trade, settlement history.
- Trace the **whole arc of origin** for each group (e.g. African-American populations back
  through their predominantly West African roots and the movements that fed into them).
- Begin weaving in the **economics** thread (historical population & output).
- Sources: Tier 3.

## Phase 4 — Deep-time / ancient genetic layer

Reach back toward common descent and dispersal.

- Haplogroups (Y-DNA, mtDNA) — the branching structure of descent.
- Ancient DNA and archaic admixture (Neanderthal / Denisovan).
- This is the longest reach back, toward a shared root and the dispersal outward.
- Sources: Tier 4.

## Phase 5 — Synthesis & micro trends

Pull all four layers together and zoom in.

- Look for **correlations**: migration ↔ culture shifts ↔ economics ↔ skills/vocation.
- Zoom into **micro mixes** (e.g. Irish–German, Irish–African-American) where distinct
  demographic and cultural patterns emerge.
- Use **culture** as a primary signal where bare data falls short.
- This is where the orienting work happens: *reading the macro heart through the micro
  vessels* — who the people of today are, where they came from, and where they are going.

---

## A note on honesty in correlation

The project's premise is **correlation, not contradiction** between Scripture and science.
To keep that intellectually honest:

- Genetic phylogenies and most-recent-common-ancestor concepts (e.g. "Y-chromosomal Adam,"
  "Mitochondrial Eve") are useful **structural parallels** to common descent — but they are
  statistical constructs, **not literal identifications**. Keep that distinction explicit.
- Prefer documented, cited data over inference. Where a claim is interpretive, mark it as
  such rather than presenting it as settled fact.
