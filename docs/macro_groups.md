# Macro Groups — America

This file fixes the **macro groupings** for the first geography (America), defined up front
so every later layer — genetics, history, ancient DNA — lines up against the same skeleton.

The groups deliberately **mirror US federal (OMB / Census) categories**. This keeps the
macro picture comparable to official data and gives the micro work (admixtures like
Irish–German or Irish–African-American) a stable scaffold to branch from.

> These are **census-style buckets for organizing data**, not statements about distinct
> kinds of people. The project's premise is one human family, many members — the categories
> are an analytical convenience, and the real work is tracing the lineage and culture
> *behind* each label.

---

## Race categories

The US standard changed in 2024, so the project must handle **two schemes** to compare
across time.

### OMB 1997 standard (used in Census 2000, 2010, 2020)

Race and Hispanic origin are asked as **two separate questions**.

1. White
2. Black or African American
3. American Indian and Alaska Native
4. Asian
5. Native Hawaiian and Other Pacific Islander
6. Some Other Race
7. Two or More Races (multiracial)

### OMB 2024 revision (SPD 15) — current

- Race and ethnicity are **combined into a single question**.
- A new **Middle Eastern or North African (MENA)** category is added (previously counted
  as White).
- This is the forward-looking standard; expect data tagged under it going forward, with
  bridging needed to compare against pre-2024 figures.

## Ethnicity (separate, under the 1997 standard)

- Hispanic or Latino
- Not Hispanic or Latino

Hispanic/Latino is an **ethnicity, not a race**, under the 1997 scheme — a person of any
race can be Hispanic. (Under the 2024 combined question this distinction collapses.) This
crosswalk matters and must be handled explicitly when joining datasets across years.

## Ancestry (the bridge to micro)

The ACS asks an open-ended **ancestry** question (e.g. German, Irish, Italian, Mexican,
Nigerian, ...). Ancestry is where the macro buckets dissolve into the **micro trends** the
project ultimately cares about — it is the natural hinge between Phase 1 (macro) and
Phase 5 (micro mixes).

---

## Origin arcs (what each macro label resolves into)

The macro label is only a starting point. For each group the project wants the **whole arc
of origin** — the historical lineage and source peoples behind today's category. Examples
of the direction this goes (to be filled in with cited data in later phases):

- **Black / African American** → predominantly **West African** roots; trace via the
  trans-Atlantic slave trade (SlaveVoyages) to specific regions and peoples, plus later
  voluntary African and Caribbean immigration.
- **White** → European source nations (German, Irish, Italian, English, ...), each with its
  own immigration waves and settlement patterns.
- **Hispanic / Latino** → blended Indigenous American, European (largely Iberian), and
  African ancestry, varying by country of origin.
- **Asian** → distinct national origins (Chinese, Indian, Filipino, Vietnamese, Korean, ...)
  with very different migration histories.
- **American Indian and Alaska Native** → the deepest-rooted populations on the continent;
  the bridge into the ancient-DNA layer (Phase 4).

Each arc gets documented and **cited** as it is built out; nothing here is asserted as
settled until backed by a source in [`../CITATIONS.md`](../CITATIONS.md).
